## Controls and onboarding

The first click on Start opens a two-page guide before skill selection; reopen it with **How to play** on the home screen or through the top **?** button on small screens. The **?** dialog also provides open-source licenses and pauses an active run until the player resumes. Small screens hide the bottom controls, turn-direction text and license button to make more room for play. Touch instructions and keyboard keys appear together, and both control methods work at any time without switching modes. The second page illustrates obstacles, transport gates, boosters, and coins. Swipe left/right or use A/D and the arrow keys to change lanes. Swipe up or press W/Up/Space to jump; swipe down or press S/Down to slide. Tap the skill button, double-tap the path, or press E to activate a charged skill. Tap the booster icon buttons or use keys 1–4 to activate purchased tools, subject to each tool's timing rules.

Extra jump inputs during a jump count as the same jump: the current arc finishes without restarting or queuing another jump. A fresh input after landing starts the next jump. Repeating a slide input restarts the slide. Switching between jump and slide immediately cancels the previous motion.

The home screen shows the saved best score. Current-run score, distance, and coin counters appear when the run starts. Home autoplay stays in the saved starting season and never enters or displays transport gates.

Short screens use a smaller home title and compact layout. Home content grows with enlarged text and scrolls with the page; the title has no separate scroll area. Long guides and store dialogs scroll within the viewport. Cramped phone landscape views ask the player to rotate to portrait. This pauses an active run, blocks game input, and keeps it paused after rotation until the player explicitly resumes. Usable tablet and desktop landscape views remain playable. Toy’s embedded viewport also checks physical screen orientation without reading the host page.

## Four seasons

Spring Commons has blossom groves, arbors and ponds; Summer Riverside uses wooden boardwalks, docks and sailboats; Autumn Avenue has markets and townhouses along a brick street; Winter Square has snow-roofed cabins, evergreens and frozen ponds. The seasonal geometry appears inside portal previews too. The last visited season is remembered in the save, including across devices when Toy cloud sync is enabled. Optional portals appear every 2,500m in varying lanes; stay in another lane to remain in the current season. A Season Pass can take you to another season at the start. A gate notice names the destination and explains how to stay in the current season. Transitions blend the current season’s colors into the destination’s colors through a tunnel, a destination title with an animated SVG TV travelling through a seasonal gate, and a protected landing. The animation pauses with the game and becomes still for reduced-motion preferences. Each season has a distinct original background score that changes with the scenery.

Summer lamps stand on wooden platforms with pilings, connected to the boardwalk and railway edges. These supports follow the fork connectors and appear in destination previews. Autumn and winter houses use enclosed pitched roofs with only exterior surfaces drawn; hidden wall caps and rear-facing roof panels cannot flash through the visible roof during running or transport.

## Posts and learning checkpoints

- Low comment cards: jump or change lanes.
- Overhead post banners: slide or change lanes.
- Tall post stacks: change lanes.
- Low spam strips: jump or change lanes; a hit brings disruptors closer.

Categories include personal attacks, group hostility, threats, privacy exposure, coordinated harassment, unsupported accusations, scam bait, and shock content. Examples are fictional and redact sensitive parts. Each checkpoint explains the boundary, offers a constructive response, and suggests a better expression. Criticism and disagreement are welcome; context matters.

Post collisions pause the lesson and freeze movement, distance, chase countdowns and boost timers. Continue after reading. A fatal hit remains fatal; a shielded hit preserves the run while consuming protection. Speed boosts and brief collision immunity pass posts without repeatedly opening dialogs. Live public case links are separate references, not claims that our fictional wording is an actual case.

During Momentum, Fresh Start, and Season Pass speed boosts, the TV softly pulses between 45% and 65% opacity to show that it can pass through obstacles. At forks, the runner smoothly steers toward an open branch about half a second before the sign (at least eight meters ahead). When both branches are open, a centered runner defaults left and an already selected right branch stays right. When one branch is a dead end, the boost chooses the other side, including steering right when the left is blocked, even if the blocked side was already selected. Even a boost activated immediately before the sign can safely turn without snapping the body sideways. The automatic turn only applies while a speed boost is active; shields and other effects do not choose a branch. Pausing freezes this pulse; the TV becomes solid again when the boost ends. Railway passengers remain solid because quiz answers still matter.

## Disruptors

Cartoon disruptive commenters approach smoothly from behind during the opening chase, then fall behind after 3.5 seconds. Returning chasers also approach gradually. An unprotected spam-strip hit during an active chase ends the run. Otherwise the first spam-strip hit brings them close for six seconds. Six clean seconds dismiss the chase. Pressing left at the left edge or right at the right edge causes a compact inward recoil and grounded recovery with 0.5 seconds of protection; quick repeated edge inputs count as one mistake and do not immediately end the run. The TV casing retains its shape through recoil and lane changes. Shields, speed boosts and temporary grace protect as before.

During Momentum, Fresh Start, or Season Pass speed boosts, lane-edge barriers and posts cause no new stumble or chase. Repeated edge inputs neither summon disruptors nor extend an existing chase, and do not consume a stacked shield. Ordinary edge recovery resumes after the boost and its brief expiry protection end.

## Coins and progress

Collect gold coins for your bank and score; 50 score per coin and 10 per meter. Short trails vary in length, contain gaps, offer coins in multiple safe lanes, change lanes, and arc above jump obstacles. Jump to collect floating Shield, Shared Rewards, Kindness Magnet, and Momentum boosters: they activate immediately at their upgrade level and refill an existing effect. Stored purchases are separate from pickups. A pickup matching your equipped skill pauses its charging until the effect ends. Normal speed increases up to 5.5×; mandatory jump/slide rows only start after a 30-second warmup.

Passing the best score saved before the run triggers a short celebration banner. A first run establishes the initial record.

Roadside power-ups are occasional opportunities: the first appears about 10–16 seconds into normal running, then later ones target a randomized 22–38 seconds of normal travel. The next suitable obstacle row can delay an opportunity slightly longer. Distances account for acceleration up to the speed cap, so power-ups do not bunch together as the game gets faster. Missing one does not trigger a replacement nearby. The minimum spacing also leaves a break after a level-three Momentum effect before the next road pickup.

## Leaderboards

On Toy, open the leaderboard to view **Today** or **This week** under the current difficulty rules. Scores appear with the nickname and avatar Toy provides. After finishing an eligible run, choose **Join leaderboard & post this score** to agree to that public display. Before this explicit choice and any required Toy permission, no score is submitted. **Not now** or declining permission keeps the score local; you can join later.

After the first submission is confirmed successful, the game saves your public-profile participation choice to a separate Toy cloud record for your account. Once that save is confirmed, later fresh eligible runs submit once automatically across devices, without asking you to post each run. Consent is not stored in the browser, and its cloud record does not replace your game progress. The old hidden-name board and its consent are not reused: returning players must join the public-profile board explicitly.

The game checks the cloud choice on loading, when you open the leaderboard, and before each automatic submission. If it cannot confirm consent, it keeps the score local. If a score posts but saving your choice fails, automatic submission stays off; retrying the choice save does not repost the score. Toy may still request renewed permission. Reloading never uploads a saved best or the previous session's last run. There is no all-time view; your saved personal best remains available separately.

You can turn off future submissions from the leaderboard at any time, including after joining or before finishing another run. This saves the disabled choice to your Toy account. If the change cannot sync, automatic submission stays off on this page and the game tells you that other devices have not received the change. Opting out does not delete an accepted score or cancel a request already sent: your score, nickname and avatar may remain visible on the current day/week boards. Toy's public SDK has no score-delete method.

Checks compare the completed run's score, distance, coins and time before submission and prevent duplicate submissions through the game. A local-storage best changed to **999,999,999,999,999** is not an eligible score; a forged submission is rejected before leaderboard SDK access. These checks reduce simple score manipulation, but do not provide trusted server verification or prevent direct SDK calls. [Technical details](../docs/LEADERBOARD.md) explain the limits.

## TV outfits

Open the store with **B** or the store button, then choose **Skins**. The **Body**, **Hats**, **Shoes** and **Effects** categories let you combine a body color with one hat, one pair of shoes and one visual effect. Your complete outfit appears in the preview. Classic TV, no hat, default shoes and no effect are free. Blossom TV, Ocean TV, Amber TV and Frost TV each cost 1,000 collected coins to unlock permanently.

Accessory prices use collected game coins, and each item is unlocked permanently:

## Saving and Toy cloud sync

Coin wallet, purchased boosters, permanent unlocks, upgrade levels, equipped skill, owned skins and accessories, equipped outfit, best score, and last season save in this browser. On Toy, cloud storage also syncs these fields across devices signed in to the same account. No real-money purchases.

When enabling cloud sync with existing browser progress, the game compares both saves before changing anything. For different saves, choose the cloud save, replace the cloud save with this device’s save, or keep playing locally. The dialog shows coins, best score, skills, levels, inventory, owned skin names, accessory ownership, each equipped outfit slot, and season so the choice can be reviewed. An empty cloud save offers upload or local-only choices. Equal saves sync without an overwrite dialog. Saves are selected as a whole, not merged, including all outfit ownership and selections.

Cloud changes are checked before a new run and applied only at a safe boundary. A conflict stops synchronization until a save is chosen; it never replaces progress in an active run. If the network or Toy sign-in is unavailable, the browser save stays usable. Use the cloud status button on the home screen to retry. Choosing local-only is remembered and can be changed with Enable cloud.

Run score, run distance, run coin counter, active boost durations, and permanent skill charge are not cloud-saved: every new run starts fresh. Earned coins still enter the saved wallet, and a completed or paused run updates the saved best score. Language, mute, and guide preferences remain per device. Outside Toy, the game uses local storage only.

Cloud writes are grouped about every three seconds and checked against the last synced revision before writing. Because Toy has no atomic compare-and-swap API, simultaneous writes from two devices can still race; detected differences require a new choice instead of guessing which device is newer.

## Forks and community railway

Obstacle rows use seeded, irregular spacing: short bursts of 2–4 intervals, mixed gaps, and occasional longer breathing spaces. Burst lengths and individual gaps vary instead of following a repeating cycle. Gaps range from 1.2 to 2.8 seconds and average about 1.44 seconds at normal speed; the first few rows are gentler. The minimum preserves room for motion recovery and keeps lane-closure tails clear of the next jump trail. Long gaps do not stack onto station or fork clearances.

Forks appear after the opening warmup: the center route always ends. About half offer both left and right turns; the rest have one dead-end branch, with either side equally likely to be blocked. A tall solid wall with an X blocks the dead-end branch. Road signs show the available turn ahead of time. On larger screens, the controls heading below the game also names the open direction in English or Chinese, scales with enlarged text, and identifies automatic steering into the sole open branch during a boost. This heading returns to its usual control label after the fork. Screen readers receive the direction and dead-end announcement too; no fork toast covers the game. Use swipes, A/D or the arrow keys to reach an open outer lane before the junction. Entering the center or the blocked branch ends the run, even with a shield. Active speed boosts automatically select an open branch. A single three-lane approach feeds separate connectors, which widen into outgoing streets where open; upcoming coins and obstacles follow the streets. The camera follows the occupied branch starting 2.2 seconds before the junction; the runner and road stay aligned through the turn. A missed fork is separate from a recoverable lane-margin stumble.

Occasional stations put the TV character in a cart for 3–4 community-guideline questions. Each ride draws different fictional, redacted questions and shuffles their answer lanes. Move with swipes, A/D or left/right arrows, or tap an answer card. The selected lane is checked when the cart reaches the answer gate. To submit sooner, tap Go inside the selected answer card or press Up/W twice within 0.3 seconds. Holding a key or double-tapping the track does not submit. Early submission judges the current choice immediately, including incorrect answers. Reading time grows with the combined character count of the question and all three answers, with four extra seconds to decide and change lanes and a minimum of 12 seconds. The timer uses whichever language needs more reading time (15 non-whitespace English characters or 5 Chinese characters per second), rounded up to a whole second. It is fixed for that question and does not shrink with running speed or language changes. Tracks and nearby seasonal scenery scroll toward the player together, including between questions. Cart speed matches normal run progression at boarding, capped at 30 meters per second, and remains stable through the ride. Temporary speed boosters do not affect this pace or the reading window. Mileage and the usual 10 points per meter accumulate; previously earned coins and score are retained. P pauses the ride, scenery, distance, and score while keeping the question visible.

A correct answer advances to the next question while keeping the cart in its selected lane. The answer positions shuffle, so players may need to move for the next question. Answer gates approach at the same visual speed as the tracks. Rail strips draw above their sleepers and deck, keeping the approach continuous. After an answer is judged, incorrect tracks end at the moving answer gate: their entire length beyond the gate disappears, while the correct track stays intact. A wrong answer sends the cart off the track, ends the run and explains that specific choice alongside the correct answer.

Station entry and exit gates show a preview of the next mode: tracks and a cart when boarding, and the current season's running path when leaving. The boarding transition clears before the first question's timer starts. After the final correct answer, the same exit gate continues toward the cart without replacing the railway with a new station or changing the passenger's pose. The cart stays in its selected lane and enters the gate; a tunnel blending the railway tint into the season’s running colors and a destination title cover the return to running. Completing the ride awards coins once, scaled by normal speed progression at boarding: 45–50 coins at base pace, rising to 106–118 coins for a 3–4-question ride at 10 km. The 1.1-second return reveal holds gameplay and boost timers; pausing also holds the transition. Ordinary obstacles, chasers and temporary boost timers wait during the ride; boosters cannot skip the questions. Home autoplay demonstrates scenery without forks, railway quizzes or travel.

Approach and exit clearances scale with speed. Forks reserve 2.2 seconds for camera anticipation, with the first post-turn row 2.8 seconds after crossing to allow the 1.4-second turn and a reaction window. Railway stations reserve 1.1 seconds before boarding (at least 14 meters), covering the one-second entry transition. During the opening warmup, normal obstacle rows continue until the station position is settled; a moving provisional station does not clear successive stretches of road. Station gates appear once their approach is prepared. Row and coin-trail extents are included in these clearances; the extra fixed-distance empty stretches have been removed.

Both fork branches retain their roadside landmarks throughout the turn, including the curved stretch behind the runner while the camera straightens. Buildings keep complete surfaces, show the faces visible from the current camera, and clip at the camera edge instead of disappearing as a whole. The distant seasonal panorama stays independent of the local junction coordinates.
